Understanding how a resistor behaves in a circuit comes down to two main concepts: how fast it uses energy, and how much total energy it uses over time. 

When you apply a voltage across a resistor, it forces electrical current through it. The resistor pushes back against this flow, and in the process, electrical energy is converted into heat. The rate at which this happens is called **Power**. To figure out the power, we look at the relationship between the applied voltage and the resistance. 

Once we know the power (how much energy is used per second), finding the total **Energy** is just a matter of multiplying that rate by the total amount of time the circuit is active. To get standard energy units like Joules (J), we just need to make sure our time is measured in seconds.


### 1. Calculating Power Dissipated
We use the relationship between Voltage ($V$) and Resistance ($R$):

* **Voltage ($V$):** 50 V
* **Resistance ($R$):** 100 $\\Omega$


**Formula:** $P = V^2 / R$


$P = 50^2 / 100$  
$P = 2500 / 100$  
**Power = 25 Watts (W)**

### 2. Calculating Total Energy Consumed
Energy is Power multiplied by time. First, we convert the time into seconds.
* **Time ($t$):** 5 minutes $\\times$ 60 = 300 seconds

**Formula:** $E = P \\times t$

$E = 25 \\text{ W} \\times 300 \\text{ s}$  
**Energy = 7500 Joules (J)**
