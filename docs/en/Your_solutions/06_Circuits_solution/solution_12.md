A transformer works by transferring electrical energy between two coils of wire. The number of loops (or "turns") in each coil determines how the voltage and current change. Because our secondary coil has fewer turns (200) than the primary coil (1000), this is a "step-down" transformer. This means it will decrease the voltage, but to keep the total energy balanced, the current will behave oppositely.

Here are the given values:
* **Primary turns:** 1000
* **Secondary turns:** 200
* **Primary voltage:** 120 V
* **Secondary current:** 3 A

### 1. Finding the Secondary Voltage

The basic rule for voltages in a transformer is that the change in voltage directly matches the physical ratio of the turns in the coils. Since we have fewer turns on the secondary side, we will get proportionally less voltage out.

**How to calculate it:** You find the secondary voltage by multiplying the primary voltage by the fraction of the secondary turns over the primary turns ($V_s = V_p \\times \\frac{N_s}{N_p}$). 

* **Calculation:** 120 V $\\times$ (200 / 1000)
* 120 V $\\times$ 0.2
* **Secondary Voltage:** **24 V**

### 2. Finding the Primary Current

Ideal transformers conserve energy, meaning the total electrical power entering the primary coil is equal to the power leaving the secondary coil. Because power is simply voltage multiplied by current, a drop in voltage means there must be a proportional increase in current, and vice versa. Therefore, the current ratio works in the exact inverse of the turns ratio. 

**How to calculate it:** To find the primary current, you multiply the secondary current by that same turns ratio we used above ($I_p = I_s \\times \\frac{N_s}{N_p}$).

* **Calculation:** 3 A $\\times$ (200 / 1000)
* 3 A $\\times$ 0.2
* **Primary Current:** **0.6 A**

### Summary
* The secondary voltage is **24 V**.
* The primary current is **0.6 A**.
