To find the voltage across a resistor in an AC circuit, we can use a foundational rule of electronics known as Ohm's Law. This law states that the voltage across a resistor is simply the current flowing through it multiplied by its resistance. 

Because this is a simple resistor, the voltage and current rise and fall exactly together (they are "in phase"). This means we don't have to change the timing or the sine wave itself; we can just multiply the overall magnitude of the current equation by the resistance value to get our voltage equation.

Here is the information given in the problem:
* **Current ($I(t)$):** $2\\sin(120\\pi t)$
* **Resistance ($R$):** $50 \\, \\Omega$

### Step-by-Step Calculation:
Now, let's apply Ohm's Law ($Voltage = Current \\times Resistance$):

1. Take the equation for the current: 
   $I(t) = 2\\sin(120\\pi t)$
2. Multiply the entire expression by the resistance ($50$):
   $V(t) = 50 \\times [2\\sin(120\\pi t)]$
3. Multiply the constants (50 and 2) together to get the maximum voltage:
   $V(t) = 100\\sin(120\\pi t)$

### Final Answer:
The equation for the voltage across the resistor is:
**$V(t) = 100\\sin(120\\pi t)$**
