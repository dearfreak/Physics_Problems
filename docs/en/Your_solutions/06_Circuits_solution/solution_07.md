### 1. Total Charge Stored
When capacitors are connected in parallel, they are placed side-by-side in the circuit. This means both capacitors experience the exact same electrical push (voltage) from the battery. To find out how much total electrical charge they can hold together, we first combine their individual storage capacities into one single total capacity.

* **Step A: Find the total capacity ($C_{\\text{total}}$)**
    For parallel connections, we simply add the individual capacities together:
    $$C_{\\text{total}} = C_1 + C_2$$
    $$C_{\\text{total}} = 4\\,\\mu\\text{F} + 6\\,\\mu\\text{F} = 10\\,\\mu\\text{F}$$
    

* **Step B: Calculate the total charge ($Q_{\\text{total}}$)**
    The basic rule of electrical storage is that charge ($Q$) equals capacity ($C$) multiplied by voltage ($V$). 
    Using our combined capacity and the $10\\,\\text{V}$ battery:
    $$Q_{\\text{total}} = C_{\\text{total}} \\times V$$
    $$Q_{\\text{total}} = 10\\,\\mu\\text{F} \\times 10\\,\\text{V} = 100\\,\\mu\\text{C}$$

**Answer for Total Charge:** $100\\,\\mu\\text{C}$ (or $1.0 \\times 10^{-4}\\,\\text{C}$)

---

### 2. Total Energy Stored
The electrical energy stored in a capacitor depends on how much total charge has accumulated and how strong the battery's push is. Since it takes work to pack the charges together, the formula calculates this accumulated energy as half of the total capacity times the voltage squared.

* **Step C: Calculate the total energy ($U_{\\text{total}}$)**
    $$U_{\\text{total}} = \\frac{1}{2} \\times C_{\\text{total}} \\times V^2$$
    Plugging in our values ($C_{\\text{total}} = 10 \\times 10^{-6}\\,\\text{F}$ and $V = 10\\,\\text{V}$):
    $$U_{\\text{total}} = \\frac{1}{2} \\times (10 \\times 10^{-6}\\,\\text{F}) \\times (10\\,\\text{V})^2$$
    $$U_{\\text{total}} = 5 \\times 10^{-6} \\times 100 = 500 \\times 10^{-6}\\,\\text{J} = 5 \\times 10^{-4}\\,\\text{J}$$

**Answer for Total Energy:** $5 \\times 10^{-4}\\,\\text{J}$ (or $0.5\\,\\text{mJ}$)
