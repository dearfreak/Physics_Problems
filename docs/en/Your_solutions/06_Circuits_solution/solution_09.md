To find the electric current, we first need to look at how it relates to charge. Current is simply the rate at which electrical charge flows over time. In calculus terms, this means we can find the current by taking the derivative of the charge function with respect to time.

## Step 1: Find the function for current
Since current ($I$) is the derivative of charge ($Q$), we take the derivative of your given function:
$I(t) = \\frac{dQ}{dt}$

Your charge function is $Q(t) = 5t^2 + 5$. Using the power rule for derivatives (multiplying the exponent by the coefficient and subtracting one from the exponent), the constant drops out and we get:
$I(t) = 10t$

## Step 2: Calculate the current at the specific time
Now that we have the formula for current, we just plug in the given time, which is **3 s**:
$I(3) = 10(3)$
$I(3) = 30$

## Final Answer:
The current at **3 s** is **30 Amperes (A)**.
