### Understanding Percentage Uncertainty
When we record a measurement, it often comes with an **absolute uncertainty** (the $\\pm$ value), which tells us the margin of error or the range within which the true value likely falls. However, an absolute uncertainty of 0.22 seconds means something very different if we are measuring a 5-second event versus a 5-hour event. 

**Percentage uncertainty** contextualizes this error. It expresses the absolute uncertainty as a fraction of the total measured value and converts it into a percentage. This allows us to quickly gauge the *relative* precision of the measurement—a lower percentage indicates a more precise measurement relative to its overall size.

The standard formula to find the percentage uncertainty is:
**Percentage Uncertainty = (Absolute Uncertainty / Measured Value) × 100%**

From the problem statement, we have the following recorded measurement for time ($t$):
* **Measured Value ($t$):** $5.45$ seconds
* **Absolute Uncertainty ($\\Delta t$):** $0.22$ seconds

**Step 1: Set up the formula**
$$\\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \\left( \\frac{0.22}{5.45} \\right) \\times 100\\%$$

**Step 2: Perform the division**
$$\\frac{0.22}{5.45} \\approx 0.040367$$

**Step 3: Convert to a percentage**
$$0.040367 \\times 100\\% \\approx 4.04\\%$$

### Final Answer:
The percentage uncertainty of the time measurement is approximately **4.04%**.
