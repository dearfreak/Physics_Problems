Given a mass $m = 10 \, \text{kg}$ oscillating according to the equation:
$$x(t) = 0.2 \cos(10\pi t)$$

## 1. Finding the Spring Constant ($k$)

The general equation for simple harmonic motion is $x(t) = A \cos(\omega t + \phi)$. By comparing this to the given equation, we can identify:
* **Amplitude ($A$):** $0.2 \, \text{m}$
* **Angular frequency ($\omega$):** $10\pi \, \text{rad/s}$



The relationship between angular frequency, mass, and the spring constant is:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \implies k = m\omega^2$$

### Calculation:
1.  **Substitute the values:**
    $$k = 10 \cdot (10\pi)^2$$
2.  **Square the angular frequency:**
    $$k = 10 \cdot 100\pi^2$$
3.  **Result:**
    $$k = 1000\pi^2 \approx 9869.6 \, \text{N/m}$$

**Answer:** The spring constant $k$ is **$1000\pi^2$ N/m** (approximately **9870 N/m**).

---

## 2. Total Mechanical Energy ($E_{total}$)

The total mechanical energy in a spring-mass system is constant and can be calculated using the maximum potential energy at the amplitude ($A$):
$$E_{total} = \frac{1}{2} k A^2$$



### Calculation:
1.  **Plug in $k$ and $A$:**
    $$E_{total} = \frac{1}{2} (1000\pi^2) (0.2)^2$$
2.  **Square the amplitude:**
    $$E_{total} = 500\pi^2 \cdot 0.04$$
3.  **Result:**
    $$E_{total} = 20\pi^2 \approx 197.4 \, \text{Joules}$$

**Answer:** The total mechanical energy is **$20\pi^2$ J** (approximately **197.4 J**).
