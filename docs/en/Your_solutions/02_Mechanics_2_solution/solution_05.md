This scenario involves a runner and a cart merging into a single moving system. We analyze this using the principle of conservation of momentum.

## 1. Finding the Final Speed ($V_f$)

In any collision where external forces (like friction) are negligible, the total momentum of the system is conserved. Since the runner jumps onto and stays on the cart, this is a **perfectly inelastic collision**.



### Given:
* **Mass of runner ($m_1$):** $70 \, \text{kg}$
* **Initial velocity of runner ($v_1$):** $3 \, \text{m/s}$
* **Mass of cart ($m_2$):** $140 \, \text{kg}$
* **Initial velocity of cart ($v_2$):** $0 \, \text{m/s}$

### Calculation:
The conservation of momentum equation is:
$$m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)V_f$$

1.  **Substitute the values:**
    $$(70 \cdot 3) + (140 \cdot 0) = (70 + 140) \cdot V_f$$
    $$210 = 210 \cdot V_f$$
2.  **Solve for $V_f$:**
    $$V_f = \frac{210}{210} = 1 \, \text{m/s}$$

**Answer:** The final speed of the cart with the runner is **1 m/s**.

---

## 2. Kinetic Energy Conservation Analysis

To determine if kinetic energy ($K$) is conserved, we compare the total kinetic energy before and after the jump.

### Before the Jump ($K_i$):
$$K_i = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_2 v_2^2$$
$$K_i = \frac{1}{2} (70)(3^2) + 0$$
$$K_i = 35 \cdot 9 = 315 \, \text{J}$$

### After the Jump ($K_f$):
$$K_f = \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) V_f^2$$
$$K_f = \frac{1}{2} (210)(1^2)$$
$$K_f = 105 \, \text{J}$$



### Explanation:
**No, kinetic energy is not conserved.** In this collision, the kinetic energy decreased from **315 J** to **105 J**, resulting in a loss of **210 J**. This energy is not "gone" from the universe but has been transformed into other forms of energy, such as:
* **Internal energy/Heat:** Generated by the friction between the runner's shoes and the cart.
* **Sound energy:** The "thud" heard when the runner lands.
* **Deformation:** Small changes in the shape of the runner's muscles or the cart's structure during impact.

In almost all real-world macroscopic collisions (especially inelastic ones), kinetic energy is lost to these non-mechanical forms.
