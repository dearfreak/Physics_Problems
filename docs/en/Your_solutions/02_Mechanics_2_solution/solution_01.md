## 1. Gravitational Dependence

The period of a simple pendulum is determined by the formula:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$

Where:
* **$T$** = Period (seconds)
* **$L$** = Length of the pendulum (meters)
* **$g$** = Acceleration due to gravity ($m/s^2$)



### Period on the Moon
Since the length $L$ remains constant, the period is inversely proportional to the square root of gravity ($T \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$). 

Given that $g_{Moon} = \frac{1}{6}g_{Earth}$, the calculation is as follows:

1.  **Ratio Setup:** $$\frac{T_{Moon}}{T_{Earth}} = \sqrt{\frac{g_{Earth}}{g_{Moon}}}$$
2.  **Substitution:** $$\frac{T_{Moon}}{4} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{1/6}} = \sqrt{6}$$
3.  **Result:** $$T_{Moon} = 4 \times \sqrt{6} \approx 9.80 \text{ seconds}$$

**Answer:** The period on the Moon would be approximately **9.80 seconds**.

## 2. Required Length for 1-Second Period on Earth

To find the specific length $L$ needed for a target period $T$, we rearrange the formula:

$$L = \frac{g T^2}{4\pi^2}$$

### Calculation
Using Earth's standard gravity ($g \approx 9.81 m/s^2$) and a target period of $T = 1 \text{ s}$:

1.  **Plugging in values:**
    $$L = \frac{9.81 \times (1)^2}{4\pi^2}$$
2.  **Simplification:**
    $$L = \frac{9.81}{39.478}$$
3.  **Result:**
    $$L \approx 0.248 \text{ meters}$$

**Answer:** The required length is approximately **0.248 meters** (or **24.8 cm**).
