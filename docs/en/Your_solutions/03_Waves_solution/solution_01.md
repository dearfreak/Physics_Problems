# Sound Wave Wavelength Calculation

To find the wavelength of a sound wave, we use the wave equation:

$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

Where:
* **$v$** = Speed of sound (m/s)
* **$f$** = Frequency (Hz)
* **$\lambda$** = Wavelength (m)

To solve for wavelength, we rearrange the formula:
$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f}$$

---

## 1. Wavelength in Air
In this scenario, the sound wave is traveling through a gas (air).

**Given:**
* Frequency ($f$) = **440 Hz**
* Speed in air ($v_{air}$) = **343 m/s**

**Calculation:**
$$\lambda_{air} = \frac{343\text{ m/s}}{440\text{ Hz}}$$
$$\lambda_{air} \approx 0.7795 \text{ m}$$

**Result:** The wavelength in air is approximately **0.78 meters**.

---

## 2. Wavelength in Water
When a wave changes mediums, its **frequency remains constant**, but its speed and wavelength change.



**Given:**
* Frequency ($f$) = **440 Hz**
* Speed in water ($v_{water}$) = **1482 m/s**

**Calculation:**
$$\lambda_{water} = \frac{1482\text{ m/s}}{440\text{ Hz}}$$
$$\lambda_{water} \approx 3.3681 \text{ m}$$

**Result:** The wavelength in water is approximately **3.37 meters**.

---

## Comparison Summary

| Medium | Speed ($v$) | Frequency ($f$) | Wavelength ($\lambda$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Air** | 343 m/s | 440 Hz | **~0.78 m** |
| **Water** | 1482 m/s | 440 Hz | **~3.37 m** |

**Note:** Because sound travels significantly faster in water (a denser medium) than in air, the wavelength must increase proportionally to maintain the same frequency.
