### 1. Capacitance ($C$)
Capacitance is the measure of how much electrical charge a system can hold for a given potential. For a parallel-plate capacitor, this depends entirely on its physical geometry.
*   **Area:** Larger plates provide more "real estate" for electrons to spread out, increasing storage.
*   **Distance:** Placing plates closer together increases the electrostatic attraction between the opposite charges, allowing more charge to be pulled onto the plates at the same voltage.

### 2. Energy Stored ($W$)
Storing energy in a capacitor is analogous to compressing a mechanical spring. Work must be done to push charges onto the plates against the repulsion of charges already there. This stored potential energy depends on the "stiffness" of the capacitor ($C$) and the "pressure" applied ($U$).

### 3. Electric Field Intensity ($E$)
The electric field is the force per unit charge. In a parallel-plate capacitor, the voltage is distributed uniformly across the gap. If you have a high voltage over a very small distance, the "intensity" or slope of the electrical potential becomes very steep.

### 4. Force of Attraction ($F$)
Since the two plates hold opposite charges, they naturally want to pull toward each other. The total force depends on the total amount of charge on each plate and the field intensity those charges "feel" from the opposite side. Interestingly, this force is also the rate at which stored energy would change if you were to pull the plates apart.

## Calculations

### 1. Capacitance ($C$)
The formula for capacitance in a vacuum/air is:
$$C = \frac{\epsilon_0 \cdot S}{d}$$
*   $\epsilon_0 \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m}$
*   $C = \frac{8.854 \times 10^{-12} \cdot 0.02}{0.005}$
*   **$C \approx 3.54 \times 10^{-11} \text{ F}$ (or $35.4 \text{ pF}$)**

### 2. Stored Energy ($W$)
The energy stored is given by:
$$W = \frac{1}{2} C U^2$$
*   $W = 0.5 \cdot (3.5416 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot (500)^2$
*   $W = 0.5 \cdot 3.5416 \times 10^{-11} \cdot 250,000$
*   **$W \approx 4.43 \times 10^{-6} \text{ J}$ (or $4.43 \mu\text{J}$)**

### 3. Electric Field Intensity ($E$)
The field intensity is the potential difference per unit distance:
$$E = \frac{U}{d}$$
*   $E = \frac{500}{0.005}$
*   **$E = 100,000 \text{ V/m}$ (or $100 \text{ kV/m}$)**

### 4. Force of Attraction ($F$)
The force can be calculated using the energy density or the charge:
$$F = \frac{W}{d} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{C U^2}{d}$$
*   $F = \frac{4.427 \times 10^{-6}}{0.005}$
*   **$F \approx 8.85 \times 10^{-4} \text{ N}$**
