To understand why a loop of wire rotates in a magnetic field, we have to look at the interaction between moving charges and magnetic forces.

### 1. Forces on the Wire
When an electric current flows through a wire in a magnetic field, it experiences a force (Lorentz force). In a rectangular loop, the current travels in different directions on opposite sides. This creates forces that pull one side up and the other side down, causing the loop to twist or rotate.

### 2. The Magnetic Moment
A current-carrying loop creates its own tiny magnetic field, effectively acting like a small bar magnet. This property is called the **magnetic moment**. The strength of this moment depends on two things: how much current is flowing ($I$) and how large the "face" of the loop is (its Area, $A$). 

### 3. Orientation and Maximum Torque
Torque is a "turning force." The amount of torque depends on the angle between the magnetic field and the face of the loop. 
*   If the field is **perpendicular** to the loop's plane, the forces just pull the loop outward or inward, and no rotation happens (zero torque).
*   If the field is **parallel** to the loop's plane (as specified in this problem), the forces are perfectly positioned to twist the loop. This results in the **maximum possible torque**.

---

## The Formula

The magnitude of the torque ($\tau$) on a current-carrying loop is determined by the magnetic moment, the field strength, and the angle ($\theta$) between the field and the normal to the loop's surface:

$$\tau = I \cdot A \cdot B \sin(\theta)$$

Where:
*   $I$ is the current.
*   $A$ is the area of the loop ($length \times width$).
*   $B$ is the magnetic field strength.
*   $\theta$ is $90^\circ$ because the field is parallel to the plane (meaning it is perpendicular to the loop's normal vector).

---

## Step-by-Step Calculation

### 1. Identify and Convert Given Values
*   Current ($I$) = $2\text{ A}$
*   Dimensions = $10\text{ cm} \times 5\text{ cm}$
*   Length in meters = $0.1\text{ m}$
*   Width in meters = $0.05\text{ m}$
*   Magnetic Field ($B$) = $0.3\text{ T}$
*   Angle ($\theta$) = $90^\circ$ (since the field is parallel to the plane)

### 2. Calculate the Area ($A$)
$$A = 0.1\text{ m} \times 0.05\text{ m} = 0.005\text{ m}^2$$

### 3. Substitute into the Formula
$$\tau = (2\text{ A}) \cdot (0.005\text{ m}^2) \cdot (0.3\text{ T}) \cdot \sin(90^\circ)$$
Since $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$:
$$\tau = 2 \times 0.005 \times 0.3$$

### 4. Compute Result
$$\tau = 0.01 \times 0.3 = 0.003\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
