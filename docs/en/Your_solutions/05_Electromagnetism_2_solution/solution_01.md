
Key logical points to consider:
1.  **Field Lines and Charge:** The number of electric field lines originating from a positive charge is proportional to the magnitude of the charge itself.
2.  **Independence of Geometry:** A fundamental principle of electromagnetism is that the total flux exiting a closed surface depends only on the total amount of charge trapped inside. It does not matter if the sphere has a radius of $1\text{ m}$, $10\text{ m}$, or if the surface is shaped like a cube; as long as the charge is fully enclosed, the total flux remains the same.
3.  **Permittivity of Free Space:** The medium (vacuum/air) has a physical constant called the vacuum permittivity ($\epsilon_0$), which represents how much "resistance" the vacuum offers to the formation of an electric field.

Because the charge is exactly at the center of a sphere, the electric field is perpendicular to the surface everywhere and has a constant magnitude. This symmetry makes it a perfect candidate for applying **Gauss's Law**.

## The Formula

According to Gauss's Law, the net electric flux ($\Phi_E$) through any closed surface is equal to the net enclosed charge ($Q_{\text{encl}}$) divided by the permittivity of free space:

$$\Phi_E = \frac{Q_{\text{encl}}}{\epsilon_0}$$

## Step-by-Step Calculation

### 1. Identify Given Values
* Enclosed Charge ($Q_{\text{encl}}$) = $+2\text{ C}$
* Permittivity of Free Space ($\epsilon_0$) $\approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ C}^2/(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2)$
* Radius ($r$) = $1\text{ m}$ (Note: As discussed in the logic section, the radius is actually irrelevant to the total flux as long as the charge is enclosed).

### 2. Substitute into the Formula
$$\Phi_E = \frac{2 \text{ C}}{8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ C}^2/(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2)}$$

### 3. Compute Result
$$\Phi_E \approx 2.259 \times 10^{11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}$$

## Final Answer
The total electric flux through the spherical surface is approximately **$2.26 \times 10^{11} \text{ V}\cdot\text{m}$** (or $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}$).
