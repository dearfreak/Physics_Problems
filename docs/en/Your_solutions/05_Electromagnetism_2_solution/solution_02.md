### 1. The Geometry of the Field
Every current-carrying wire generates a magnetic field that circles around it. The strength of this field weakens as you move further away. In this scenario, since we are looking at the **midway point**, the distance from each wire is exactly half of the total separation.

### 2. The Right-Hand Rule (RHR)
This is the most critical part of the logic. Even though the currents are in opposite directions, the fields they create at the center do not necessarily cancel out.
*   **Wire 1 (Current Up):** Using the right-hand rule (thumb in direction of current), your fingers curl "into" the page on the right side of the wire.
*   **Wire 2 (Current Down):** Positioned to the right of the midpoint, a downward current also creates a field that points "into" the page on its left side.

Because both wires contribute a magnetic field pointing in the same direction at the midpoint, the total field is the **sum** of the two, not the difference.

### 3. Magnetic Permittivity
The constant $\mu_0$ (permeability of free space) represents how easily a magnetic field can form in a vacuum. It acts as the scaling factor between the current and the resulting field strength.

---

## The Formula

The magnitude of the magnetic field $B$ at a distance $r$ from a long straight wire carrying current $I$ is given by:

$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$$

For the total field at the midpoint ($B_{\text{total}}$):
$$B_{\text{total}} = B_1 + B_2$$

---

## Step-by-Step Calculation

### 1. Identify Given Values
*   Current ($I_1 = I_2$) = $5\text{ A}$
*   Total Distance ($d$) = $10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m}$
*   Distance to midpoint ($r$) = $5\text{ cm} = 0.05\text{ m}$
*   Permeability constant ($\mu_0$) = $4\pi \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$

### 2. Calculate the field from one wire
$$B_1 = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A})(5\text{ A})}{2\pi(0.05\text{ m})}$$
The $2\pi$ and $4\pi$ simplify:
$$B_1 = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{0.05}$$
$$B_1 = 2 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}$$

### 3. Sum the fields
Since $B_1$ and $B_2$ are identical in magnitude and direction:
$$B_{\text{total}} = 2 \times (2 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}) = 4 \times 10^{-5} \text{ T}$$
