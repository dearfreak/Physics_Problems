The Biot-Savart Law is the fundamental law used to determine the magnetic field generated by a constant electric current. While Ampere's Law is useful for symmetric, infinite wires, the Biot-Savart Law allows us to look at a specific, small piece of a wire (a "current element") and see how it contributes to the field at a distant point.

### 1. Current and Length Relationship
The strength of the magnetic field ($B$) is directly proportional to both the amount of current ($I$) flowing through the wire and the length of the segment ($dL$). More electrons moving through more wire space creates a stronger disturbance in the surrounding magnetic field.

### 2. The Inverse Square Law
Just like gravity or electric fields from point charges, the magnetic field from a current element follows an inverse square law. This means that as you double the distance ($r$) from the wire, the strength of the magnetic field doesn't just halve; it drops to one-fourth of its original value.

### 3. The Role of the Angle
The direction and magnitude of the field depend on the angle ($\theta$) between the wire segment and the line pointing toward the observation point. The field is strongest when the point is perpendicular to the wire ($90^\circ$) and zero if the point is directly in line with the wire ($0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$). In this problem, because the segment is **perpendicular**, we get the maximum possible field for this distance and current.

### 4. Permeability Constant
The constant $\mu_0$ (permeability of free space) scales the physical units of current and distance into the unit of magnetic flux density (Teslas).

---

## The Formula

The magnitude of the magnetic field contribution ($dB$) from a small current element $I \, dl$ is given by the Biot-Savart Law:

$$B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I \, \Delta L \sin(\theta)}{r^2}$$

Where:
* $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space ($4\pi \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$).
* $I$ is the current.
* $\Delta L$ is the length of the small segment.
* $r$ is the distance from the segment to point $P$.
* $\theta$ is the angle between the segment and the vector to point $P$.

---

## Step-by-Step Calculation

### 1. Identify Given Values
* Current ($I$) = $3\text{ A}$
* Segment Length ($\Delta L$) = $0.1\text{ m}$
* Distance ($r$) = $0.2\text{ m}$
* Angle ($\theta$) = $90^\circ$ (so $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$)
* $\mu_0 / 4\pi = 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$

### 2. Substitute into the Formula
$$B = (10^{-7}) \frac{(3 \text{ A}) (0.1 \text{ m}) (1)}{(0.2 \text{ m})^2}$$

### 3. Simplify the Arithmetic
$$B = 10^{-7} \times \frac{0.3}{0.04}$$
$$B = 10^{-7} \times 7.5$$
$$B = 7.5 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$$
